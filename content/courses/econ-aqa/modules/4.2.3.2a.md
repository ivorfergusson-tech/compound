## Measuring unemployment

The UK has two main measures, which you met in 4.2.1.

- **The Labour Force Survey (LFS):** a survey that uses the International Labour Organization definition. It counts people who are out of work, have looked for work recently and can start soon.
- **The claimant count:** the number claiming unemployment-related benefits.

The LFS is usually higher, as it includes people who don't or can't claim. The latest LFS rate is 4.7%, for June to August 2026, according to the ONS.

## Voluntary and involuntary unemployment

**[[voluntary unemployment|Voluntary unemployment]]** means people choose not to take a job at the going wage, perhaps as they'd gain little over benefits.

**[[involuntary unemployment|Involuntary unemployment]]** means people are willing to work at the going wage, but can't find a job.

## The four types

**[[seasonal unemployment|Seasonal unemployment]]** occurs when demand for labour changes at certain times of year. Think of farm work, tourism and ski instructors.

**[[frictional unemployment|Frictional unemployment]]** is the short-term unemployment of people moving between jobs. Imperfect information about vacancies makes it last longer. It exists even in a healthy economy.

**[[structural unemployment|Structural unemployment]]** arises from long-term changes in the economy, when workers' skills or location don't match the jobs on offer. Think of mining or manufacturing towns after their main industry has declined, or jobs lost to new technology. It's linked to occupational and geographical immobility.

**[[cyclical unemployment|Cyclical unemployment]]** (or demand-deficient unemployment) happens when AD is too low in a downturn, so firms need fewer workers. It's linked to a **negative output gap**.

## Why the type matters

Different causes need different policies.

| Type | Possible policy |
|---|---|
| Frictional | Better job information, shorter searches |
| Structural | Retraining, help with moving, regional policy |
| Cyclical | Raising AD, with lower interest rates or more government spending |
| Seasonal | Little needed, though some firms can diversify |

More demand won't help a miner with no skills for the jobs on offer. Retraining won't help much in a deep recession with few vacancies.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain the difference between structural and cyclical unemployment. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Structural unemployment comes from long-term changes in the structure of the economy. Cyclical unemployment comes from a lack of aggregate demand in a downturn.

**Application:** Closing the UK's coal mines caused structural unemployment in mining areas. The fall in spending in 2008 and 2009 caused cyclical unemployment across the country.

**Analysis:** With structural unemployment, jobs exist in the economy, but the workers lack the skills or are in the wrong place, so the problem can last for years. With cyclical unemployment, there are too few jobs overall, as firms need fewer workers because of low demand, so jobs should return as AD recovers.
:::

Why it scores: it gives a cause and an example of each, and it explains why they differ in how long they last.
