## What demand means

**[[demand|Demand]]** is the quantity of a good or service that consumers are **willing and able to buy** at a given price, in a given period.

"Able" matters. Wanting a sports car isn't demand if you can't pay for it. Economists call this **effective demand**: a want backed by the ability to pay.

## The demand curve

A **[[demand curve]]** shows the relationship between price and quantity demanded. It normally slopes **downwards**: as price falls, quantity demanded rises.

Three reasons, the last linking back to 4.1.2:

- **Income effect:** a lower price means your money goes further, so you can afford more.
- **Substitution effect:** the good is now cheaper than its alternatives, so people switch to it.
- **Diminishing marginal utility:** each extra unit gives less satisfaction, so people only buy more if the price is lower.

## Movement or shift?

This is a distinction exam questions often rely on.

- A change in the good's **own price** causes a **movement along** the curve. Use the words **extension** (price falls, more demanded) and **contraction** (price rises, less demanded).
- A change in **anything else** causes a **shift** of the whole curve. Right means more is demanded at every price. Left means less.

## What shifts the demand curve

**PIRATES** is a handy memory aid:

- **P**opulation: more people, or a change in age mix, means more buyers.
- **I**ncome: higher incomes raise demand for most goods (normal goods) and reduce it for inferior goods (more in the next lesson).
- **R**elated goods: the prices of **[[substitutes]]** and **[[complements]]**. If tea gets dearer, demand for coffee rises. If printers get cheaper, demand for ink rises.
- **A**dvertising: successful marketing raises demand.
- **T**astes and fashion: changing preferences, health news, trends.
- **E**xpectations: if people expect prices to rise soon, they buy now.
- **S**easons: more ice cream in summer, more heating oil in winter.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain, using a diagram, why a rise in the price of cinema tickets might increase the demand for streaming subscriptions. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Goods are substitutes if they meet similar wants, so a rise in the price of one increases demand for the other.

**Application:** Cinema trips and streaming subscriptions are both forms of film entertainment.

**Analysis:** As cinema tickets become more expensive, some consumers cut down on cinema trips and switch to watching at home. More subscriptions are demanded at every price, so the demand curve shifts to the right from D1 to D2, and the quantity demanded rises at the original price.
:::

Why it scores: it names the relationship (substitutes), gives the reason consumers switch, and uses the language of the diagram. In the exam, you'd draw and label the shift.
