## What government failure means

**[[government failure|Government failure]]** occurs when government intervention in the economy leads to a misallocation of resources. It can make an existing problem worse, or create a new one.

The test is the same as for market failure: are resources being put to their best use? Remember that spotting a market failure only shows that intervention **might** help.

## Sources of government failure

**Inadequate information.** Governments don't know the exact size of an externality or how consumers will respond, so they may set a tax too high or too low.

**Conflicting objectives.** Goals can clash. A fuel duty cut helps drivers and firms, but it adds to pollution and reduces revenue. A minimum wage may help low earners and cost some jobs.

**Administrative costs.** Running a policy costs money. Monitoring and enforcing it may cost more than the problem is worth.

**Market distortions.** Governments can create distortions, as well as remove them. A subsidy can keep an inefficient firm alive and move resources to the wrong places. Price controls stop prices from doing their job, causing shortages or surpluses.

**Unintended consequences.** People respond in ways that weren't planned:

- High taxes on cigarettes may encourage smuggling.
- Rent controls may lead landlords to sell up or to neglect their property.
- A ban can create a black market.

Politics can add to these. Politicians may prefer policies that win votes in the short term, and lobbying by firms may shape what's done.

## Weighing the two failures

The key question is whether the result of intervening would be better than the result of leaving the market alone.

The possibility of government failure means that, **even when there's market failure, intervention won't necessarily improve economic welfare.** It depends on:

- How serious the market failure is.
- How much the government knows.
- How well the policy is designed, and whether it can be changed if it fails.
- The costs of doing it.

This doesn't mean governments should never act. Failed policies can be improved, and doing nothing also has costs. A strong answer compares the likely costs and benefits of both.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how a government subsidy could lead to government failure. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Government failure occurs when intervention leads to a misallocation of resources. A subsidy is a government payment that lowers a firm's costs.

**Application:** A government might subsidise an industry to protect jobs in a declining region.

**Analysis:** If it lacks full information, it may set the subsidy too high, so that too much is produced. The subsidy can also weaken the pressure on firms to cut costs, so an inefficient industry survives. Resources stay in that industry and don't move to better uses, and the money has an opportunity cost, as it could have been spent elsewhere. So resources could be allocated worse than before.
:::

Why it scores: it names sources of failure and takes them through to the misallocation.
