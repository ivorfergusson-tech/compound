## What supply means

**[[supply|Supply]]** is the quantity of a good or service that firms are **willing and able to sell** at a given price, in a given period.

## The supply curve

A **[[supply curve]]** shows the relationship between price and quantity supplied. It normally slopes **upwards**: as price rises, quantity supplied rises.

Why?

- **The profit incentive:** a higher price implies higher profit, which gives firms the **incentive to expand production**. Resources also move towards the more profitable good.
- **Rising costs:** making more can cost more per unit, as firms pay overtime or use less suitable resources, so they need a higher price to make it worthwhile.
- **New firms:** higher prices can attract new producers.

You should also know that **under perfect competition, the supply curve is the marginal cost curve**. A firm will supply one more unit only if the price at least covers what that unit costs to make. You'll see why in 4.1.5.

## Movement or shift?

It's the same rule as for demand:

- A change in the good's **own price** causes a **movement along** the curve: an **extension** (price up, more supplied) or a **contraction** (price down, less supplied).
- A change in **anything else** causes a **shift**. Right means more is supplied at every price. Left means less.

:::

## What shifts the supply curve

Most shifts come down to the **cost of production**. If costs fall, firms can supply more at every price.

- **Costs of factors of production:** wages, raw materials, energy. Dearer inputs shift supply left.
- **Technology:** better methods lower costs and shift supply right.
- **Productivity:** more output per worker lowers the cost per unit.
- **Indirect taxes:** a tax on a good acts like a cost, so supply shifts left.
- **Subsidies:** a government payment to producers shifts supply right.
- **Number of firms:** more firms in the market means more supply.
- **Natural shocks:** a poor harvest, floods or disease shift supply left, a good harvest right.
- **Prices of related goods:** if lettuce gets more profitable, a farmer may grow less of something else.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain, using a diagram, how a government subsidy to electric car makers would affect the supply of electric cars. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** A subsidy is a payment from the government to producers, which reduces their costs of production.

**Application:** If each electric car maker receives a payment for every car it makes, the cost to the firm of producing each car falls.

**Analysis:** Lower costs mean firms are willing and able to supply more cars at every price, as each is now more profitable. So the supply curve shifts to the right, from S1 to S2. With demand unchanged, the equilibrium price falls and the quantity bought and sold rises.
:::

Why it scores: it defines the subsidy, links it to costs and then profit, and shows the result on the diagram, including the effect on price and quantity.
