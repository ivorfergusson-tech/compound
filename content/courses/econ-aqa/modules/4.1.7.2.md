:::callout idea
Absolute poverty means lacking the basics needed to live, while relative poverty means falling well behind the living standards of others in your society, and each has its own causes and effects.

## Absolute and relative poverty

**[[absolute poverty|Absolute poverty]]** means having too little income to afford basic needs, such as food, clothing and shelter. It doesn't depend on how anyone else is doing.

**[[relative poverty|Relative poverty]]** means having much less than the typical person in your society. The UK usually measures this as a household income below **60% of median income**.

Key differences:

- As an economy grows, absolute poverty tends to fall.
- Relative poverty can stay the same or rise, even as everyone gets richer, if the poorest don't keep up with the median.

The latest UK official statistics, for the year to March 2025, put **20% of people** and **27% of children** below the 60% line, after housing costs. Before housing costs, the figures are 16% and 21%.

## Causes of poverty

- **Unemployment:** no wage income.
- **Low pay:** low skills, or insecure and part-time work. Many people in poverty live in a working household.
- **Sickness, disability or old age:** less ability to work.
- **Family structure:** a single earner, or more children to support.
- **High living costs:** especially housing.
- **Low benefits:** benefits that don't keep up with prices.
- **Discrimination and poor education,** which limit opportunities.
- **Where people live:** jobs and pay differ by region.

## Effects of poverty

**On individuals and families:**

- Worse physical and mental health, and shorter lives.
- Poorer education, which limits future earnings.
- Stress, debt and a lack of security.

**On the economy and society:**

- Less human capital, so lower productivity.
- More spending on benefits and health care, and less tax revenue.
- More crime and social tension.
- A **poverty cycle:** children who grow up in poverty are more likely to be poor as adults.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain why relative poverty might rise, even if absolute poverty falls. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Absolute poverty means not having enough income for basic needs. Relative poverty means having an income below a share of the median, such as 60%.

**Application:** Suppose incomes at the bottom rise by 2% while median income rises by 5%.

**Analysis:** The poorest can buy more than before, so fewer of them lack the basics and absolute poverty falls. But the median has risen faster, so the 60% line has moved up by more than their incomes. More households are now below that line, so relative poverty rises. This shows that the two measures can move in opposite directions.
:::

Why it scores: it defines both, uses numbers and shows how the same change affects each measure.
