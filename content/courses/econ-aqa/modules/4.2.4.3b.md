A change in Bank Rate spreads through the economy by several channels, including borrowing, saving and the exchange rate, and when rates can't go lower, central banks have other tools.

## The transmission mechanism

The **[[transmission mechanism]]** is the way that a change in Bank Rate affects the economy. Follow a **rise** in Bank Rate:

**1. Market interest rates rise.** Banks pass this on to mortgages, loans and savings.

**2. Consumption falls.** Borrowing costs more, saving pays more, and those on variable-rate mortgages have less to spend.

**3. Investment falls.** Fewer projects are worthwhile, and firms expect weaker sales.

**4. Asset prices fall.** House and bond prices tend to fall, so wealth falls.

**5. The exchange rate rises.** See below.

**6. Confidence and expectations change.** A credible rise can lower expected inflation.

So AD shifts left, the multiplier adds to the effect, and inflation falls. A cut in Bank Rate works the other way round.

## Interest rates and the exchange rate

When UK interest rates rise relative to those abroad, UK assets pay a better return. Foreign savers buy pounds to put their money in the UK. Demand for the pound rises, so it **appreciates**.

## How the exchange rate affects AD and the objectives

A **stronger pound** makes UK exports dearer abroad and imports cheaper in the UK.

- **AD:** net exports fall, so AD falls.
- **Inflation:** falls, as imported goods and raw materials get cheaper, and AD is lower.
- **Growth and unemployment:** can worsen, as exporters lose sales.
- **Current account:** worsens in the short run.

A **weaker pound** does the reverse: it raises AD, but it adds to inflation. So the exchange rate links monetary policy to all the main objectives.

## Quantitative easing

When Bank Rate is already very low, cutting it further may do little. **[[quantitative easing|Quantitative easing (QE)]]** is when a central bank creates new money and uses it to buy financial assets, mainly government bonds.

Here's how it works:

1. The Bank buys bonds from pension funds, insurers and others.
2. Demand for bonds rises, so **bond prices rise and yields fall**. Long-term interest rates fall.
3. Sellers now hold money, and they may buy other assets, which raises prices and wealth.
4. Borrowing becomes cheaper, so spending rises, and AD rises.

The Bank of England began QE in 2009, after the financial crisis, and used it again during the pandemic.

**Quantitative tightening (QT)** is the reverse: reducing those holdings. In September 2026, the MPC voted to reduce its stock of bonds held for monetary policy to zero, over several years.

**Limits:** it may raise inflation and asset prices, it helps those who own assets the most, which can widen inequality, and banks may not pass on the extra money as loans.

## Funding for Lending and forward guidance

**Funding for Lending** was a scheme that began in 2012. The Bank offered banks cheap funding, on condition that they kept lending to households and firms. The aim was to tackle the shortage of credit after the crisis.

**Forward guidance** means telling the public how interest rates are likely to change in future. If firms and households believe that rates will stay low, they're more likely to borrow and invest. It works through **expectations**, so it costs little, but it depends on being believed.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how a cut in Bank Rate could increase aggregate demand. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Bank Rate is the interest rate set by the MPC, which influences other interest rates. AD = C + I + G + (X − M).

**Application:** A cut would tend to lower variable mortgage rates and loan rates for UK firms.

**Analysis:** Households with mortgages have more to spend, and saving pays less, so consumption rises. Firms find investment cheaper to fund, so it rises too. Lower rates also make UK assets less attractive, so the pound may fall, which makes exports cheaper and imports dearer, so net exports rise. All three parts of AD increase.
:::

Why it scores: it covers three channels, each linked to a part of AD.
