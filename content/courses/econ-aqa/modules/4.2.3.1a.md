## Short-run and long-run growth

**[[short-run growth|Short-run growth]]** is a rise in real GDP, usually as the economy uses its spare capacity. It's often driven by demand.

- On a **PPF**, the economy moves from a point inside the curve towards the curve.
- On an **AD/AS** diagram, AD shifts right along SRAS.

**[[long-run growth|Long-run growth]]** is a rise in the productive capacity of the economy. It's the **trend rate of growth** of real output over time.

- On a **PPF**, the curve shifts outwards.
- On an **AD/AS** diagram, LRAS shifts right.

Try moving AD to see the first, and capacity to see the second.

## What drives growth

**Demand-side determinants of short-run growth** are the parts of AD: consumption, investment, government spending and net exports. Lower interest rates, tax cuts or rising confidence and exports can all raise spending.

**Supply-side determinants of the long-run trend rate** include:

- **More or better labour:** population, migration, education and training.
- **More capital:** investment in machines and infrastructure.
- **Technology and innovation.**
- **Better use of resources:** factor mobility, competition and enterprise.
- **Institutions and incentives.**

Investment does both jobs: it adds to AD now and adds to capacity later.

## Benefits of economic growth

**For individuals:** higher incomes and living standards, more jobs, better public services.

**For the economy:** more tax revenue without higher tax rates, less spending on benefits, and more profit for investment. Governments can pay for health and education more easily.

**For the environment:** richer countries can afford clean technology. Growth can also come from better ideas, and not just from using more resources.

## Costs of economic growth

**For individuals:** longer hours, stress, and job losses as industries change. The gains may be shared unequally.

**For the economy:** demand-led growth above trend can cause inflation, and more imports can worsen the current account.

**For the environment:** pollution, loss of habitats, climate change and using up finite resources.

## Sustainable growth

**[[sustainable growth|Sustainable growth]]** means growth that can continue over time without causing serious problems, such as high inflation, unmanageable debt or environmental damage for future generations.

Growth built on rising debt or asset prices may not last. Growth that uses up natural resources leaves less for the future. Sustainability is about the kind of growth, not only its rate.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Using an AD/AS diagram, explain the difference between short-run and long-run economic growth. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Short-run growth is a rise in real GDP using spare capacity. Long-run growth is a rise in the productive capacity of the economy.

**Application:** After a recession, UK output might rise as unemployed workers go back to work. Over decades, it rises mainly because of better technology and skills.

**Analysis:** On an AD/AS diagram, short-run growth is shown by AD shifting right, so real output rises along SRAS towards LRAS, and the price level rises too. Long-run growth is shown by LRAS shifting right, so the economy's capacity grows, which allows output to rise without necessarily pushing up prices.
:::

Why it scores: it defines both, then shows each one with a different curve on the diagram.
