:::callout idea
Markets sit on a spectrum from perfect competition to pure monopoly, and a firm's power over price depends on how many rivals it has, how different its product is and how easily new firms can enter.
:::

## The spectrum of competition

A **market structure** is the set of features of a market that shape how firms behave. Economists place markets on a spectrum:

| Structure | Number of firms | Product | Entry | Power over price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[perfect competition|Perfect competition]] | Very many | Identical | Free | None |
| [[monopolistic competition|Monopolistic competition]] | Many | Slightly different | Easy | A little |
| [[oligopoly|Oligopoly]] | A few big firms dominate | Identical or different | Hard | Quite a lot |
| [[monopoly|Pure monopoly]] | One | Unique | Blocked | The most |

The two ends are models. Very few real markets are perfectly competitive, and pure monopolies are rare. Most real markets sit somewhere in between.

## How to tell structures apart

Three factors do most of the work.

**Number of firms.** More firms usually means more competition. What matters is how much of the market each firm has. A market with 100 firms is an oligopoly if four of them make most of the sales.

**[[product differentiation|Product differentiation]].** This means making a product seem different from its rivals, by design, quality, branding or service. The more different a product seems, the fewer close substitutes it has, so the less elastic its demand and the more power the firm has over price.

**Ease of entry.** This depends on [[barriers to entry]]. If entry is easy, high profits attract new firms, which compete those profits away. If entry is hard, firms can keep high profits for longer.

## Why structure matters

Structure affects conduct, and conduct affects performance:

- **Structure:** number of firms, differentiation, barriers.
- **Conduct:** how firms set prices, advertise and compete.
- **Performance:** profits, efficiency, quality and innovation.

So a change in structure, such as a merger or new technology that lowers barriers, can change what consumers pay and get. Later lessons in this section model each structure in turn.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain two factors that distinguish oligopoly from perfect competition. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Market structures are distinguished by factors such as the number of firms, the degree of product differentiation and the ease of entry.

**Application:** In the UK grocery market, a few large supermarkets have most of the sales, whereas in the model of perfect competition there are very many small firms.

**Analysis:** With very many firms, none can affect the market price, while a few large firms can each influence it. Barriers to entry also differ. Free entry means abnormal profits are competed away in perfect competition, while high barriers in oligopoly let the big firms keep them for longer.
:::

Why it scores: it names two factors and explains what each does to prices or profit.
