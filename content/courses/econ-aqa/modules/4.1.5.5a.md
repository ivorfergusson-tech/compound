## The main characteristics

[[oligopoly|Oligopoly]] means a few large firms dominate the market. Many firms may exist, but a handful make most of the sales. UK examples include groceries, mobile networks and banking.

Its main features:

- **High concentration:** a few firms have most of the market.
- **Barriers to entry**, which keep new firms out.
- **Interdependence:** each firm's best move depends on what its rivals do.
- **Uncertainty:** no firm knows for sure how rivals will react.

Oligopolies can be very different from each other. They differ in the number of firms, the degree of product differentiation and the ease of entry. Cement is close to identical, whereas cars and phones are heavily branded.

## Structure or conduct

Oligopoly can be defined in two ways.

- **By market structure:** the number of firms, their shares, barriers and differentiation. A high concentration ratio points to oligopoly.
- **By market conduct (behaviour):** how firms act. Do they watch and react to each other, or collude? Do they avoid price competition?

A market could look like an oligopoly by structure, yet behave competitively. That's why economists look at both.

## Concentration ratios

A **[[concentration ratio]]** shows the combined market share of the largest firms in a market. A **5-firm concentration ratio** of 80% means the five biggest firms have 80% of the market.

To calculate an n-firm ratio:

1. Find the n largest firms.
2. Add up their shares.

If you are given sales, divide their combined sales by the total market sales, then multiply by 100.

| Firm | Sales (£m) |
|---|---|
| A | 400 |
| B | 300 |
| C | 200 |
| D | 60 |
| E | 40 |

The total is £1,000m. The 3-firm ratio is (400 + 300 + 200) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = **90%**.

Concentration ratios don't tell you everything. They don't show how firms behave, whether a market is global or local, or how evenly shares are split among the top firms.

## Interdependence and uncertainty

If you run a firm with many rivals, one firm's choice hardly affects you. In an oligopoly, it does. A price cut by one supermarket can take sales from the others, so they may react.

This makes decisions hard. A firm has to work out what rivals will do, and they're doing the same about it. This is why oligopolists may prefer to keep prices stable and avoid a fight.

## Collusive and non-collusive oligopoly

- **Non-collusive oligopoly:** firms compete and act on their own, though they still take account of each other.
- **Collusive oligopoly:** firms agree, openly or tacitly, to limit competition.

[[collusion|Collusion]] allows firms to act like a **single monopolist**, setting prices and output to maximise their **joint profits**.

It can be:

- **Overt:** a formal agreement between firms.
- **Tacit:** no agreement, but firms understand each other and follow the same pattern.

## Cooperation and collusion

These aren't the same thing.

- **[[cooperation|Cooperation]]** means firms working together in ways that can benefit consumers or the public, such as agreeing common technical standards, sharing research or joining a safety scheme.
- **Collusion** means working together to reduce competition, such as fixing prices or sharing out markets. This harms consumers, and most forms are illegal in the UK.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain why interdependence is important in oligopoly. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Interdependence means that a firm's best decision depends on the actions of its rivals. Oligopoly is a market dominated by a few large firms.

**Application:** If one of the big supermarkets cuts the price of its milk, the others may lose customers.

**Analysis:** Because each firm has a large share, a move by one has a clear effect on the sales of the others, and they may respond. So a firm can't judge the effect of its price change without guessing their reaction. If rivals match a cut, it gains few customers and earns less on each sale. This uncertainty can lead firms to avoid price changes, or to collude.
:::

Why it scores: it defines the term, uses a real kind of market and explains what follows from it.
