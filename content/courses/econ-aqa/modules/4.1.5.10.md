## Guess first

Market A sells a simple product as cheaply as it can be made. Market B charges more, but its product gets much better every year.

:::guess {"prompt":"Which market would you rather have as a consumer? Does your answer change if you think 20 years ahead?","kind":"text","id":"econ-aqa:4.1.5.10-guess"}
:::

:::callout idea
Static efficiency is about using resources well at one point in time, and dynamic efficiency is about improving over time, and the market structure that's best for one isn't always best for the other.
:::

## Static and dynamic efficiency

**[[static efficiency|Static efficiency]]** is efficiency at a point in time, with technology and resources given. It covers productive and allocative efficiency.

**[[dynamic efficiency|Dynamic efficiency]]** is about how resource use improves **over time**, through better products and lower costs.

## The two kinds of static efficiency

**[[productive efficiency|Productive efficiency]]** means producing at the lowest possible cost.

- Condition: output is at the **lowest point of the ATC curve**.

**[[allocative efficiency|Allocative efficiency]]** means making the mix of goods that people want most.

- Condition: **price = marginal cost**. The value consumers put on the last unit equals the cost of making it.

If P > MC, consumers value one more unit above its cost, so too little is being made.

## What drives dynamic efficiency

Dynamic efficiency is influenced by:

- **Research and development:** new products and methods.
- **Investment in human capital:** training and skills.
- **Investment in non-human capital:** new machines and infrastructure.
- **Technological change:** better ways of doing things.

These need finance, and they need a reason to take the risk. So profits and market power can help. A firm making only normal profit may have little to spend, and a firm with no rivals may have little reason to spend it.

## Comparing the market structures

| Structure | Productive | Allocative | Dynamic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect competition (long run) | Yes | Yes | Likely weak: normal profit and no secrets |
| Monopolistic competition (long run) | No: excess capacity | No: P > MC | Limited: normal profit, some product change |
| Oligopoly | Depends on behaviour | Unlikely: P > MC | Often strong: profit and rivalry together |
| Monopoly | Not guaranteed | No: P > MC | Could be strong, or weak if there's no pressure |

The table suggests a trade-off. Perfect competition does best on static efficiency, yet market power may do better over time.

Be careful with this. A monopolist's large economies of scale could give it lower costs than small competitive firms, even if it isn't at the lowest point of its own ATC curve.

## Using conduct and performance indicators

To compare real markets, look at what firms **do** and how they **perform**.

**Conduct indicators** include how firms set prices, how much they spend on advertising, and whether they collude or compete.

**Performance indicators** include:

- Profit margins.
- Prices against costs.
- Spending on research and rates of innovation.
- Quality, choice and consumer satisfaction.

A market with high research spending and falling prices may perform well, even if it's concentrated.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain why a monopoly may be dynamically efficient but not statically efficient. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Static efficiency refers to productive and allocative efficiency at a point in time. Dynamic efficiency refers to improvements in resource use over time.

**Application:** A drug firm with a patent can earn high profits on a new medicine.

**Analysis:** It restricts output and sets price above MC, so it's allocatively inefficient. It may not produce at the lowest point of ATC either. But its abnormal profit can pay for research, and barriers such as patents mean it keeps the gains. This gives it the means and the reason to develop new products, so it could be dynamically efficient while being statically inefficient.
:::

Why it scores: it treats both types of efficiency and explains how the same profit supports innovation.
