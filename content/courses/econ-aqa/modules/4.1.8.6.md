## Imperfect and asymmetric information

**[[imperfect information|Imperfect information]]** means buyers or sellers don't have all the facts they need to decide well.

**[[asymmetric information|Asymmetric information]]** is a special case: one side knows more than the other.

- A seller knows more about a used car than the buyer.
- A pension adviser knows more than the client.
- A person buying insurance knows more about their own risks than the insurer.

These can lead to market failure, because:

- People buy too much of things they overrate, or too little of things they underrate. So resources go to the wrong uses.
- Those with the information can exploit it, by overcharging or selling poor products.
- Trust can fall, and buyers may offer less for all goods because they can't spot the good ones. Sellers of good products may then leave the market, so that it shrinks.

## Monopoly and monopoly power

You've seen the analysis in 4.1.5. A firm with monopoly power can restrict output and raise its price, so that:

- **Price is above marginal cost:** the firm is allocatively inefficient, and too few resources are used in making the good.
- **There's a deadweight loss:** units valued above their cost aren't made.
- **Costs may be higher,** as there's less pressure to cut waste.

Remember the other side: large firms may have economies of scale and may fund innovation. That's why policy doesn't simply try to break up every large firm.

## Immobility of factors of production

Market forces only work if resources can move to where they're wanted. **[[factor immobility|Immobility of factors]]** stops this.

**Occupational immobility** means workers can't easily change jobs, as their skills don't fit. A coal miner can't simply become a software engineer.

**Geographical immobility** means workers can't easily move to where the jobs are, perhaps due to housing costs or family ties.

Capital can be immobile too: a specialist factory can't easily make something else, and land has a fixed location.

The result is market failure. When demand changes, resources stay stuck in old uses, leaving unemployment in one place and job vacancies in another. The economy stays inside its PPF.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how asymmetric information can lead to market failure. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Asymmetric information exists when one party to a transaction knows more than the other. Market failure occurs when resources are misallocated.

**Application:** Someone selling a pension product knows much more about its charges and risks than a typical customer.

**Analysis:** Since the buyer can't judge the product, they may pay too much or buy something unsuitable. Sellers have a reason to exploit this. So demand doesn't reflect the true value of the product, and more resources are allocated to it than would be if buyers were well informed. This misallocation of resources is market failure.
:::

Why it scores: it links the information gap to behaviour, and behaviour to resource allocation.
