## Absolute and comparative advantage

**[[absolute advantage|Absolute advantage]]** means being able to produce **more** of a good from the same resources.

**[[comparative advantage|Comparative advantage]]** means being able to produce a good at a **lower opportunity cost**.

The idea of comparative advantage is usually linked with David Ricardo. It shows that what matters isn't who's best, but who gives up the least.

## A numerical example

Each country has the same number of workers. This shows the output per worker:

| | Cars | Wheat |
|---|---|---|
| UK | 8 | 4 |
| France | 6 | 6 |

**Opportunity cost of 1 car:**

- UK: 4 ÷ 8 = **0.5 wheat**.
- France: 6 ÷ 6 = **1 wheat**.

The UK gives up less wheat for each car, so it has the **comparative advantage in cars**.

**Opportunity cost of 1 wheat:**

- UK: 8 ÷ 4 = **2 cars**.
- France: 6 ÷ 6 = **1 car**.

France has the **comparative advantage in wheat**.

## Specialisation raises total output

Suppose each country has 10 workers, and splits them equally.

| | Cars | Wheat |
|---|---|---|
| UK (5 + 5) | 40 | 20 |
| France (5 + 5) | 30 | 30 |
| **Total** | **70** | **50** |

Now each country moves workers towards its comparative advantage. The UK puts 9 workers on cars and 1 on wheat. France puts 1 worker on cars and 9 on wheat.

| | Cars | Wheat |
|---|---|---|
| UK (9 + 1) | 72 | 4 |
| France (1 + 9) | 6 | 54 |
| **Total** | **78** | **58** |

Both totals are now higher than the 70 and 50 from before, so there's more of both goods to share out. Trade at a price between the two opportunity costs lets both countries end up better off.

## Assumptions and limits

The model assumes no transport costs, constant costs, full employment and factors that can move between uses. In reality, workers can't easily switch, and transport and tariffs add costs. Comparative advantage can also change as countries invest in skills and technology.

## Other benefits of trade

- **Economies of scale:** firms can sell to larger markets, so their average costs fall.
- **More competition:** this lowers prices and pushes firms to innovate.
- **More choice** for consumers.
- **Access to resources** that a country lacks.
- **Spread of technology and ideas.**

## Costs of trade

- **Job losses** in industries that can't compete.
- **Reliance on other countries,** for example for energy.
- **Damage to the environment** from transport and from weak rules abroad.
- **Harm to infant industries** that can't yet compete.
- **Unequal gains** within countries.

## Changes in the UK's pattern of trade

The UK's trade has changed for several reasons:

- **Comparative advantage has shifted,** with manufacturing moving to lower-cost countries, and the UK specialising more in services, such as finance. In the three months to August 2026, the ONS estimated a **goods deficit of £59.1 billion**, and a **services surplus of £51.1 billion**.
- **Emerging economies,** such as China and India, have grown, so trade has shifted towards them.
- **Leaving the EU** has changed trade with the UK's nearest markets, which face more checks and paperwork, though the EU is still a major partner.
- **New trade deals:** the UK joined the CPTPP group of countries in 2024, and a UK-India free trade agreement came into force on 15 July 2026.
- **Exchange rates and technology** also play a part.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain the difference between absolute and comparative advantage. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Absolute advantage is being able to produce more of a good with the same resources. Comparative advantage is being able to produce a good at a lower opportunity cost.

**Application:** Suppose a worker in country A makes 10 cars or 10 tonnes of wheat, and a worker in country B makes 4 cars or 8 tonnes of wheat.

**Analysis:** A has an absolute advantage in both goods, as it makes more of each. But a car costs A 1 tonne of wheat, and it costs B 2 tonnes, so A has the comparative advantage in cars. A tonne of wheat costs B only half a car, against 1 car in A, so B has the comparative advantage in wheat. So both can gain by specialising, even though A is better at both.
:::
