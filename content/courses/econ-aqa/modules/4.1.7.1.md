## Income and wealth

- **[[income|Income]]** is a **flow**: money received over a period, such as wages, rent, interest, dividends and benefits.
- **[[wealth|Wealth]]** is a **stock**: the value of assets held at a point in time, such as property, pensions, savings and shares.

The two are linked. Wealth produces income, and saved income builds wealth.

## What shapes the distribution?

**Income** depends on:

- Wages, skills and education.
- Whether people are in work, and for how many hours.
- Ownership of assets, which pay rent, interest and dividends.
- Taxes and benefits.
- Age, region and discrimination.

**Wealth** depends on:

- Inheritance.
- Income, which allows people to save.
- House prices and share prices.
- Entrepreneurship and luck.

Wealth tends to build on itself. Those with assets can earn a return on them and pass them on.

## Equality and equity

These words are easily confused.

- **[[equality|Equality]]** means everyone has the same share. It's about measurement.
- **[[equity|Equity]]** means fairness. It's a value judgement.

A distribution could be unequal yet seen as fair, if the differences reflect effort or skill. It could be equal yet seen as unfair, if people who work harder get no more. The degree of inequality can be **measured**, but whether it's equitable can't be, and people's views differ.

## The Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient

A **[[lorenz curve|Lorenz curve]]** shows the cumulative share of income held by the cumulative share of people, from the poorest up. If everyone had the same income, it would be the 45-degree **line of perfect equality**. The further the curve bows away from that line, the greater the inequality.

The **[[gini coefficient|Gini coefficient]]** puts a number on this. It's the area between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve, as a share of all the area below the line.

- **0** means perfect equality.
- **1** means perfect inequality, where one person has everything.

It's sometimes shown as a percentage, from 0 to 100. You won't have to calculate it, but you'll need to interpret it.

## The UK distribution

Some recent figures from the Office for National Statistics:

- The Gini for household **disposable income** was 32.7% in the year to March 2025.
- The richest fifth of households had about **5.6 times** the average disposable income of the poorest fifth.
- Taxes and benefits narrow the gap. In the year to March 2024, the Gini fell from about 48% on income before them to about 27% after them.
- **Wealth is far less equal than income.** In Great Britain in April 2020 to March 2022, the Gini for household wealth was about 0.59, and the wealthiest 10% of households held about 41% of all wealth.

## Benefits and costs of more or less equality

| More equal | More unequal |
|---|---|
| **Benefits:** more spent on health and education by those on low incomes, less poverty, stronger communities | **Benefits:** a stronger incentive to work, train and take risks, more saving to fund investment |
| **Costs:** weaker incentives, and higher taxes may discourage work and enterprise | **Costs:** poverty, wasted talent, social tension, and less demand since the rich spend less of each extra pound |

Excessive inequality is both a **cause and a consequence of market failure**. Monopsony and imperfect information can cause it. It can also be a cause when poorer people can't afford education or health care, so those are under-consumed.

People's value judgements about what's fair decide which of these they stress, and so which policies they favour.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain the difference between equality and equity in the distribution of income. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Equality means that income is shared out equally among people. Equity means that the distribution is fair.

**Application:** The UK's Gini coefficient measures how unequal disposable income is, but it can't say whether that level is fair.

**Analysis:** Equality can be measured, so it's a positive matter. Equity depends on a value judgement. Someone might see an unequal distribution as equitable if higher incomes reward skill and effort, while others might see it as inequitable if it reflects inheritance or discrimination. So a more equal distribution isn't necessarily a more equitable one.
:::

Why it scores: it separates what's measured from what's judged, and shows how the two can diverge.
