## Growth and development

**Economic growth** is a rise in real output.

**[[economic development|Economic development]]** is a wider improvement in people's lives: better health, education and living standards, more freedom and choice, and less poverty.

Growth can help development, by paying for schools and clinics. But it doesn't guarantee it. If gains go to a few, or if growth harms health and the environment, development may lag.

## Characteristics of less-developed economies

Less-developed economies differ a lot, but common features include:

- **Low income** per head.
- **High poverty and inequality.**
- **Reliance on primary products,** such as farming or mining.
- **Rapid population growth** and a young population.
- **Poor health and education,** with low life expectancy.
- **Weak infrastructure** and low investment.
- **A large informal economy,** outside official records.

## Measuring development

The main measure is the **[[human development index|Human Development Index (HDI)]]**, published by the United Nations. It combines three dimensions:

- **Health:** life expectancy at birth.
- **Education:** expected and mean years of schooling.
- **Standard of living:** gross national income per head, at PPP.

It runs from **0** to **1**, and a higher number means more development.

**Strengths:** it goes beyond income, it's simple, and it's easy to compare.

**Limits:** it leaves out inequality, the environment and freedom, and it uses averages. Data can be poor in the countries where it matters most.

## Other indicators

- **Poverty rates:** the World Bank's international line for extreme poverty is $3.00 a day, updated in June 2025.
- **Inequality:** the Gini coefficient.
- **Health:** infant mortality and access to clean water.
- **Education:** literacy and school enrolment.
- **Economic structure:** the share of people working in farming.
- **Access to technology and energy.**

Using a range of indicators gives a fuller picture than any one figure.

## Factors affecting growth and development

- **Investment:** more capital raises productivity. Low incomes mean low saving, which means low investment. This is a poverty trap for a country.
- **Education and training:** raise human capital.
- **Health:** a healthy workforce is more productive.
- **Natural resources:** these can help, though they can also lead to corruption and reliance on one export.
- **Institutions:** stable government, law and property rights.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain the difference between economic growth and economic development. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Economic growth is a rise in real GDP. Economic development is a wider improvement in living standards, including health, education and poverty.

**Application:** An oil-rich country might see GDP rise quickly, while most people lack clean water and schools.

**Analysis:** Growth measures output, so it can rise even if few people benefit. Development depends on how income is used and shared. If oil revenue pays for schools and clinics, growth supports development. If it goes to a small elite, GDP rises with little development. The HDI captures this better than GDP, since it includes health and education.
:::

Why it scores: it defines both, then shows how one can happen without the other.
