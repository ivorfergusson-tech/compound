## What this course is

At some point, a lot of money decisions land on you: your first payslip, a savings account, a credit card offer, a workplace pension, maybe an app that makes trading look like a game. Most people make those decisions with no training at all, then learn from their mistakes. This course is the training first.

It's free, it's for anyone in the UK, and it assumes you know nothing. By the end you'll understand your own money, know how investing really works, see why most traders lose (and practise safely with pretend money), and be able to talk about banks, markets and the Bank of England with confidence.

## The four strands

The course has 32 modules in four strands. The strands take turns, so all four grow together:

- 💷 **Money:** your own money: income, tax, budgeting, credit, ISAs, pensions.
- 📈 **Investing:** shares, bonds, funds, risk, and building a long-term portfolio.
- 🕯️ **Trading:** how markets move minute to minute, charts, and risk management. **Paper only**: you practise with pretend money, never real money.
- 🏦 **Industry:** who does what in finance, how banks make money, and how it's all regulated.

Each strand starts from zero and ends with a **capstone**: your own financial plan, a portfolio, a review of your paper trades, and a stock pitch.

## The route

**Beginner (modules 1–9):** how money works; shares, bonds and funds; investing vs trading vs gambling; a map of the finance industry; your income and tax; how stock markets work; orders, spreads and leverage; how banks make money; budgeting and savings.

**Intermediate (modules 10–21):** risk and diversification; starting paper trading; central banks and interest rates; credit and debt; index funds and fees; reading charts; investment banking; ISAs; financial statements; indicators; fund managers, hedge funds and private equity; pensions.

**Advanced (modules 22–28):** valuation; risk management for traders; bonds and the yield curve; pay, student loans and living costs; behavioural biases; testing a trading strategy; derivatives.

**Capstones (modules 29–32):** your own plan, a portfolio, a trade review, and regulation plus a stock pitch.

There are four **checkpoint tests**, after modules 8, 16, 24 and 32. Each mixes questions from everything so far.

## How each module works

Every module is one page. Work down it:

1. **Recall check** (from module 2): three or four quick questions on earlier modules. The ones you got wrong before come back first, because that's how memory sticks.
2. **Before you read:** a question to guess at. Guessing first, even badly, makes the answer stick.
3. **The lesson**, in short sections, with **quick checks** along the way. These aren't scored.
4. **Calculators and charts** wherever numbers matter. Change the numbers and watch what happens.
5. **In my own words:** explain the idea as if to a friend. If you can't, you've found the bit to reread.
6. **The module quiz:** 6–8 questions. Score **60%** to pass and unlock the next module. You can retry as often as you like, and each retry shuffles the questions and changes the numbers. Your best attempt counts.

A module takes about 30–40 minutes. One a week is a good pace, but go at whatever speed suits you.

## Words you'll meet first

- [[money|Money]]: anything people accept in exchange for goods and services. In the UK that's pounds, mostly as numbers in bank accounts.
- [[asset|Asset]]: something you own that's worth money: savings, shares, a car, a house.
- [[interest|Interest]]: the price of using money. Savers earn it and borrowers pay it. Module 1 covers it properly.
- [[inflation|Inflation]]: prices in general rising over time, so the same £1 buys less. Module 1 again.
- [[market|Market]]: anywhere buyers and sellers meet to agree a price. A stock market is one where people buy and sell shares in companies (module 6).

Tap any underlined word in the course to see what it means.

## A word on safety

This course is **education, not financial advice**. It explains how things work so you can make your own decisions; it can't know your situation. The rules are for the UK (England, Wales and Northern Ireland, with Scotland flagged where income tax differs), and every figure that changes shows when it was last checked.

Trading in this course is **paper only**. Be wary of anyone, online or off, who promises big, guaranteed returns.
