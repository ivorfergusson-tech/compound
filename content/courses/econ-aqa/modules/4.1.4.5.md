:::callout idea
As firms grow, cost per unit usually falls because of economies of scale, until the firm gets so big that it's hard to run, and costs can rise again.
:::

## Economies of scale

**[[economies of scale|Economies of scale]]** are the **falling long-run average costs** that come from increasing the scale of production.

**Internal** economies come from the firm's own growth. A useful list:

- **Technical:** big machines cost less per unit of output. Doubling a container's size more than doubles its volume.
- **Purchasing (bulk buying):** big orders win discounts.
- **Managerial:** big firms can hire specialist managers.
- **Financial:** banks see big firms as safer, so they can borrow more cheaply.
- **Marketing:** one national advert costs less per unit sold.
- **Risk-bearing:** a wide range of products means one failure won't sink the firm.

**[[external economies of scale|External economies of scale]]** come from the growth of the **whole industry**, often in one area, and lower costs for all firms in it. Examples are skilled workers trained nearby, specialist suppliers moving in, and better transport links. Think of the finance firms clustered in the City of London.

## Diseconomies of scale

**[[diseconomies of scale|Diseconomies of scale]]** are the **rising long-run average costs** that come from a firm getting too big.

- **Communication:** messages pass through many layers and get lost or delayed.
- **Coordination:** it's hard to keep many sites and departments working together.
- **Motivation:** workers in a huge firm can feel like a number, so effort falls.

External diseconomies can occur too: if an industry crowds into one area, rents and wages can be pushed up.

## Returns to scale and economies of scale

The two ideas are linked:

- **Increasing returns to scale** mean output rises faster than inputs, so cost per unit falls: economies of scale.
- **Decreasing returns to scale** mean cost per unit rises: diseconomies of scale.

But they aren't identical. Returns to scale are about **physical** inputs and output. Economies of scale are about **costs**. For example, bulk buying lowers costs without any change in returns to scale.

## The long-run average cost curve

The traditional **LRAC** curve is U-shaped:

- It falls as output rises, because of **economies of scale**.
- It may be flat in the middle, with constant returns.
- It rises at high output, because of **diseconomies of scale**.

Some economists argue the curve is **L-shaped**: costs fall with scale, and then flatten out and stay low, because good management and modern communication can avoid diseconomies.

External economies shift the whole LRAC curve **down**. External diseconomies shift it **up**.

## Minimum efficient scale

The **[[minimum efficient scale]]** (MES) is the **lowest output at which long-run average cost is at its lowest**. It's where the falling part of the LRAC curve first flattens.

It matters for how an industry looks:

- **High MES compared with market size** (car making, aircraft): only a few big firms can reach low costs. This creates a **[[barriers to entry|barrier to entry]]**, as a small new firm would have much higher costs. The market tends towards a few large firms.
- **Low MES** (hairdressing, cafés): many small firms can survive.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain why a large supermarket chain may have lower average costs than a small independent shop. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Economies of scale are falls in long-run average cost as a firm increases its scale of production.

**Application:** A national supermarket chain orders huge quantities of each product from suppliers.

**Analysis:** Bulk buying gives it strong bargaining power, so it pays less per unit than a small shop, which buys in small amounts. It can also spread the cost of national advertising and distribution over a far bigger output. So its cost per unit is lower, which lets it charge less or earn a bigger margin.
:::

Why it scores: it defines the term, picks specific economies that fit a supermarket, and explains how each one lowers cost per unit.
