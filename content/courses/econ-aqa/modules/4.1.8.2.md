:::callout idea
Market failure means a market leads to a misallocation of resources, either by providing too much or too little of something, or by not existing at all.
:::

## What market failure means

**[[market failure|Market failure]]** occurs whenever a market leads to a **misallocation of resources**. The market may work smoothly, with supply meeting demand, but the outcome isn't the best one for society.

Remember that resources are allocated efficiently when the value of the last unit to society equals its cost to society. Market failure means that too much or too little is produced.

## Complete and partial market failure

**Complete market failure** happens when no market exists at all, which is called a **[[missing market]]**. Nothing is supplied, though people would benefit from it. Street lighting is the usual example.

**Partial market failure** happens when a market exists but gives the wrong outcome: the wrong amount or the wrong price. Cigarettes and private education are both supplied, but arguably in the wrong amounts.

## The causes of market failure

| Cause | What goes wrong | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Public goods | Not provided, due to free riders | Flood defences, street lighting |
| Negative externalities | Too much is produced | Pollution from a factory |
| Positive externalities | Too little is produced | Vaccination |
| Merit goods | Too little is consumed | Education, health care |
| Demerit goods | Too much is consumed | Cigarettes, gambling |
| Monopoly and other market imperfections | Prices too high, output too low | A dominant firm |
| Inequalities of income and wealth | Some can't afford what they need | Poor housing |

## Why this matters

Market failure is the main economic case for government intervention, which is the subject of 4.1.8b. Be careful with one thing, though: spotting a market failure doesn't prove that a government could do better. It may fail too.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain the difference between complete and partial market failure. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Market failure occurs when a market leads to a misallocation of resources. Complete market failure means there's a missing market. Partial market failure means a market exists but misallocates resources.

**Application:** Street lighting is an example of complete failure, whereas the market for tobacco is an example of partial failure.

**Analysis:** A private firm wouldn't supply street lighting, because it can't stop non-payers from benefiting, so no market forms although people value it. Tobacco is supplied, but smokers don't take account of the costs to others, so too much is bought. In the first case the resource allocation is wrong because nothing is supplied, and in the second because the quantity is wrong.
:::

Why it scores: it defines both types and gives each one a reason, not just an example.
