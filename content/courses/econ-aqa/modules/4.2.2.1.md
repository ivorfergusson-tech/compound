:::callout idea
One person's spending is another's income, so money flows round the economy, and national income rises when more enters that flow than leaves it.

## What national income measures

**[[national income|National income]]** measures the total income earned in an economy over a year, which is the same as the value of its output. You met it in 4.2.1, and GDP is the most common measure.

- **Nominal income** is measured at current prices.
- **Real income** is adjusted for inflation, so it shows what income can actually buy.

**Real national income** is an indicator of economic performance, because it rises only when the economy produces more.

## The circular flow

Picture a simple economy of households and firms.

- Households supply factors of production, such as labour, to firms.
- Firms pay households for these: wages, rent, interest and profit. That's **income**.
- Households spend that income on the goods and services that firms make. That's **expenditure**.
- The value of those goods and services is **output**.

So three ways of measuring the same flow must give the same answer:

**National income = national output = national expenditure**

You don't need the detail of how the accounts are built.

## Injections and withdrawals

Real economies have other flows.

**[[withdrawals|Withdrawals]]** (or leakages) are money leaving the flow:

- **S**avings
- **T**axes
- **M** imports

**[[injections|Injections]]** are money entering the flow:

- **I**nvestment by firms
- **G**overnment spending
- **X** exports

Use the sliders to see what each does to national income.

:::chart circular {"j":100,"w":100}

- If **injections > withdrawals**, national income **rises**.
- If **injections < withdrawals**, national income **falls**.
- If **injections = withdrawals**, national income is in **equilibrium**.

## Equilibrium and full employment income

**Equilibrium income** is the level at which there's no pressure for income to change, as injections equal withdrawals.

**Full employment income** is the level at which the economy is using all its resources, with no demand-deficient unemployment.

These needn't be the same. An economy can settle at an equilibrium **below** full employment, with idle workers and factories, if total spending is too low. This was a central idea of John Maynard Keynes.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how a rise in exports would affect national income. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Exports are an injection into the circular flow of income. National income rises when injections exceed withdrawals.

**Application:** Suppose demand rises for UK-made cars abroad.

**Analysis:** Foreign spending enters the UK's circular flow, so UK firms earn more. They produce more and hire more workers, which raises household incomes. Households then spend some of that income, so other firms' sales rise. National income goes on rising until withdrawals, such as saving and taxes, have grown enough to equal injections again.
:::

Why it scores: it follows the money round the flow, and it says where the process ends.
