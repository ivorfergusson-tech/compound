## What monopoly means

A **pure [[monopoly]]** is the only seller in a market, so the firm is the industry. Pure monopoly is rare. What's more common is **[[monopoly power]]**: the ability of a firm to set prices above the competitive level, without being a sole seller.

Firms in monopolistic competition and oligopoly are also **price makers**, with varying degrees of monopoly power.

## What gives a firm monopoly power?

- **Barriers to entry:** such as economies of scale, patents or control of a key resource.
- **The number of competitors:** the fewer the rivals, the fewer substitutes.
- **Advertising:** this builds loyalty, which makes demand less elastic.
- **Product differentiation:** the more unique a product seems, the less elastic its demand.

## The monopoly diagram

The monopolist's demand curve is the market demand curve, which slopes down. To sell more it must lower its price, so **MR lies below AR**.

1. **Output:** where MC = MR.
2. **Price:** go up to the AR curve at that output.
3. **Cost:** ATC at that output.
4. **Abnormal profit:** (AR − ATC) × output, the shaded box.

Barriers stop new firms entering, so this profit can last into the long run.

## Advantages and disadvantages of monopoly

**Disadvantages:**

- **Higher prices and lower output** than a competitive market, so consumers lose out.
- **Allocative inefficiency:** P is above MC.
- **Productive inefficiency:** a monopolist needn't produce at the lowest point of ATC.
- **X-inefficiency:** with no rivals, a firm may let its costs rise, and it has less reason to innovate.
- **Less choice** for consumers.

**Advantages:**

- **Economies of scale:** a large firm may have much lower costs, which can mean lower prices than many small firms could offer.
- **Research and development:** abnormal profit can pay for it, and the promise of patent protection gives a reason to innovate.
- **Natural monopoly:** where costs are so high that one firm can supply the market more cheaply than several, as with water pipes.
- **Stability:** a secure firm can invest for the long term.
- **Competing abroad:** a large domestic firm may be better able to compete in world markets.

Whether a monopoly is good or bad depends on how far it uses its scale to benefit consumers, and on how contestable and well-regulated its market is.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain why a monopolist's price is likely to be higher than in a competitive market. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** A monopolist is the only seller in a market and maximises profit where MC = MR.

**Application:** On a monopoly diagram, MR lies below AR because the firm must cut its price to sell more.

**Analysis:** Because MR falls faster than AR, the firm reaches MC = MR at a lower output than the point where P = MC, which is the outcome in a competitive market. It then charges the price consumers will pay for that output, which is above MC. So output is restricted and price is higher, and barriers to entry stop rivals from undercutting it.
:::

Why it scores: it uses the diagram logic, compares with the competitive outcome and says why the price holds.
