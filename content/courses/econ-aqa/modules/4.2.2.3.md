Aggregate demand is made up of consumption, investment, government spending and net exports, and each depends on its own factors, such as income, interest rates and confidence.
:::

## What AD is

**Aggregate demand** is total planned spending on an economy's output at a given price level:

**AD = C + I + G + (X − M)**

## Consumption (C)

**[[consumption|Consumption]]** is spending by households on goods and services. It's the largest part of AD in the UK. It depends on:

- **Disposable income:** the main factor. When income rises, people spend more.
- **Interest rates:** lower rates make borrowing cheap and saving less attractive.
- **Confidence:** worried people spend less.
- **Wealth:** rising house or share prices make people feel better off.
- **Availability of credit.**
- **Taxes and benefits,** which affect disposable income.

## Saving

**[[saving|Saving]]** is income that's not spent. It depends on many of the same factors, in reverse:

- **Income:** better-off people can save more.
- **Interest rates:** higher rates reward saving.
- **Confidence:** in uncertain times, people save more as a precaution.
- **Age and pensions:** people often save in middle age for retirement.
- **Inflation expectations:** if prices are expected to rise, people may spend now.

## Saving and investment are different

These words mean different things in economics.

- **Saving** is income **not spent** on consumption. It's done by households and firms.
- **Investment** is spending on **capital goods**, such as machines and factories. It's done mainly by firms.

Buying shares isn't investment in this sense, as it creates no new capital. Banks can turn savings into loans for investment, but the two are separate decisions.

## Investment (I)

**Investment** depends on:

- **Interest rates:** lower rates make projects cheaper to fund.
- **Confidence and expectations:** firms invest if they expect sales to hold up.
- **Profits:** retained profit is a major source of funds.
- **Technology:** new methods create reasons to invest.
- **Government policy,** such as tax allowances.
- **Rising demand:** see the accelerator below.

## The accelerator

The **[[accelerator]]** is the idea that investment depends on the **rate of change** of output or income. When demand is growing, firms need more machines to keep up, so investment rises. If demand merely stops growing, firms need no extra machines, so investment can fall, even though demand is still high.

So small changes in demand can bring large swings in investment. You won't have to do calculations on this.

## Government spending (G)

**G** depends on political choices and on the state of the economy. It includes spending on health, education, defence and infrastructure. Benefit payments are transfers, not part of G.

## Net exports (X − M)

- **Exports** bring in spending from abroad.
- **Imports** are spending that goes abroad.

**Net exports** depend on:

- **The exchange rate:** a weaker pound makes exports cheaper and imports dearer.
- **Incomes at home and abroad:** rising UK incomes raise imports, and rising incomes abroad raise exports.
- **Relative inflation and productivity,** which affect competitiveness.
- **The quality of UK goods and services.**

If net exports rise, AD rises, so output and employment rise. If imports grow faster than exports, net exports reduce AD. That helps explain why a current account deficit can hold back growth.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how a fall in interest rates could increase aggregate demand. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** AD = C + I + G + (X − M). Interest rates are the cost of borrowing and the reward for saving.

**Application:** If the Bank of England cuts Bank Rate, mortgage and business loan rates tend to fall.

**Analysis:** Households with variable-rate mortgages have more disposable income, and borrowing is cheaper, so consumption rises. Saving is less attractive too. Firms find that more investment projects are worthwhile, since the cost of funds has fallen, so investment rises. Both are parts of AD, so AD shifts to the right.
:::

Why it scores: it shows two routes to higher AD, each with its reasoning.
