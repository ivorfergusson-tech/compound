## What profit is

**[[profit|Profit]] = total revenue − total costs**

If costs are greater than revenue, the firm makes a **loss**.

Economists and accountants count costs differently. An economist's costs include **opportunity cost**: what the owner's time and money could have earned elsewhere.

## Normal and abnormal profit

**[[normal profit|Normal profit]]** is the **minimum** profit needed to keep a firm in its current business. It's the reward just big enough to stop the owner moving their resources elsewhere. Because of that, economists count normal profit as a **cost of production**.

**[[abnormal profit|Abnormal profit]]** (also called **supernormal profit**) is any profit **above** normal profit.

So:

- **TR = TC** (with TC including normal profit): the firm makes normal profit.
- **TR > TC:** abnormal profit.
- **TR < TC:** a loss, in the economist's sense.

Back to the café. If the £30,000 salary was your next best option, normal profit is £30,000. Earning £25,000 means you're making a loss in the economist's sense, even though the accountant's figure is positive.

## The role of profit in a market economy

Profit does four important jobs:

- **Signal:** high profits show that consumers want more of something. Losses show they want less.
- **Incentive:** profit encourages firms to produce, take risks and cut costs.
- **Allocating resources:** resources move from loss-making to profitable uses, so the economy makes more of what people want.
- **Finance:** retained profit pays for investment and research, without borrowing.

Profit can also reward things society doesn't want. A firm with market power may earn high profits by charging too much. You'll weigh this up in 4.1.5.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain the difference between normal and abnormal profit. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Normal profit is the minimum return needed to keep a firm in its current market, and is treated as a cost of production. Abnormal profit is any profit above that.

**Application:** If a business owner could earn £40,000 in their next best job, then £40,000 is roughly their normal profit. If the business makes £55,000, then the abnormal profit is £15,000.

**Analysis:** Normal profit covers the opportunity cost of the owner's resources, so there is no reason for them to leave. Abnormal profit means the resources are earning more here than elsewhere, which attracts other firms if they can enter the market.
:::
