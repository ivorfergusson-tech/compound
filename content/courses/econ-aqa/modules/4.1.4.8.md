## Invention and innovation

- **[[invention|Invention]]** is creating a new product or process: the original idea, often a scientific discovery or a design.
- **[[innovation|Innovation]]** is turning that idea into something that's used or sold. It's bringing an invention to market.

An invention sitting in a lab earns nothing. Firms and economies gain from innovation.

Innovation comes in two forms:

- **Product innovation:** new or better goods and services.
- **Process innovation:** new or better ways of making them.

## Effects on production, productivity and costs

Technological change can affect:

- **Methods of production:** robots on car lines, 3D printing, automated warehouses.
- **Productivity:** more output for each worker or machine.
- **Efficiency:** less waste, which can improve productive efficiency.
- **Costs of production:** lower average costs, which shift a firm's cost curves down and shift supply to the right.

There can be costs too. New technology is expensive to buy, workers need retraining, and some jobs disappear.

## New products, new markets and lost markets

Technological change can:

- **Develop new products:** smartphones, electric cars, weight-loss drugs.
- **Develop new markets:** app stores, streaming, online delivery.
- **Destroy existing markets:** video rental shops, film for cameras, and much of the market for CDs.

## Creative destruction

**[[creative destruction|Creative destruction]]** is the idea, from the economist Joseph Schumpeter, that new technology and new firms **replace** old ones, and that this is how capitalist economies grow. The destruction of old firms and jobs is the price of new ones, and resources move to better uses.

It raises a trade-off. Consumers and the economy gain in the long run, while workers and firms in the old industry can lose out heavily in the short run.

## Technology and the structure of markets

Technology can change how competitive a market is, in both directions.

**More competitive:**

- It can lower barriers to entry. A small seller can now reach customers worldwide through a website.
- Online price comparison gives consumers better information.

**Less competitive:**

- A firm with the best technology can dominate. **Patents** can stop rivals from copying it.
- Online platforms often gain from network effects: the more users they have, the more useful they are. So a few firms can come to dominate.

Firms with high profits can also afford more research, so market power and innovation are linked, as you'll see in 4.1.5.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how technological change can both create and destroy markets. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Technological change is the development of new products and methods of production. Creative destruction is the process by which new technology replaces old products and firms.

**Application:** Music streaming was made possible by faster internet connections and smartphones.

**Analysis:** It created a new market in which consumers pay a subscription and not for each album. As consumers switched, demand for CDs fell, and many music shops closed. So the same change created a new market and shrank an old one, and resources moved from the old industry to the new.
:::

Why it scores: it uses the key term, gives a real case and shows both sides in one chain.
