## Merit and demerit goods

A **[[merit good]]** is a good that society believes people should consume more of than they'd choose to in a free market. Examples include education, health care and vaccinations.

A **[[demerit good]]** is a good that society believes people should consume less of. Examples include cigarettes, alcohol and gambling.

The classification depends on a **value judgement**. Someone has to decide what's good for people. One person sees a bet as harmless fun, while another sees it as a harmful habit. So these labels are normative.

## Why they're misallocated

There are two main reasons.

**1. Externalities in consumption.** Merit goods often bring external benefits, and demerit goods often bring external costs. For example, an educated person benefits others through their work, and smoking harms others through passive smoking and costs to the NHS.

**2. Imperfect information.** People may misjudge the true private costs and benefits to themselves.

- **Merit goods:** people underestimate the long-term benefits. A teenager may not see what staying in education will be worth to them.
- **Demerit goods:** people underestimate the harm. The risks of smoking lie far in the future, and they may not be well understood.

This is why the problem exists even with no external effects. People may simply not act in their own long-term interests.

## Not the same as externalities

Merit and demerit goods often have externalities in consumption, but the two ideas are different. **Not all products that bring externalities in consumption are merit or demerit goods.**

For example, a neighbour's beautiful front garden gives others a positive externality, but we wouldn't usually call it a merit good. Merit and demerit goods carry a judgement about what's good for the person consuming them.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain why education may be under-consumed in a free market. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** A merit good is one that society believes people should consume more of than they would in a free market. Education has positive externalities and is affected by imperfect information.

**Application:** A 16-year-old deciding whether to stay in education may focus on the wages they could earn right away.

**Analysis:** First, they may underestimate the long-term private benefits, such as higher pay, because of imperfect information. Second, they don't take account of the external benefits, such as a more productive workforce. So MSB is above MPB, and at the market outcome consumption is below the social optimum. If education had to be paid for, this would be worse for those on low incomes.
:::

Why it scores: it uses both causes, and links them to MPB and MSB.
