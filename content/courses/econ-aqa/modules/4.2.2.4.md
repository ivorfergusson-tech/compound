:::callout idea
A change in spending can lead to a larger change in national income, because one person's spending becomes another's income, and the more of each extra pound people spend, the bigger the effect.
:::

## AD and economic activity

AD plays a major role in the level of economic activity. When spending rises and there's spare capacity, firms sell more, produce more and hire more. When spending falls, the reverse happens. This is why governments and central banks try to manage AD.

## The multiplier

The **[[multiplier]]** process is why an initial change in expenditure can have a larger effect on national income.

Follow a £10m injection:

1. The builders receive £10m as income.
2. They spend some of it in local shops, which raises the shopkeepers' income.
3. The shopkeepers spend some of that, which raises someone else's income.
4. This goes on, with each round smaller than the last.

The rounds get smaller because some income leaks out at each stage, through saving, taxes and imports.

## The marginal propensity to consume

The **[[marginal propensity to consume|marginal propensity to consume (MPC)]]** is the share of each extra £1 of income that's spent on consumption.

If you get £100 more and spend £80, your MPC is 0.8.

**Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)**

With an MPC of 0.8, the multiplier is 1 ÷ 0.2 = **5**.

**Change in national income = multiplier × initial change in spending**

So a £10m injection gives a £50m rise in income.

## Why the MPC matters

The **higher** the MPC, the more of each pound stays in the flow, so the **larger** the multiplier. A low MPC means that more leaks out at each round, so the effect dies away quickly.

The multiplier works in both directions. A fall in spending can lead to a larger fall in income.

## Limits of the multiplier

The real-world effect may be smaller than the formula suggests:

- **Near full capacity,** more spending raises prices more than output.
- **Time lags:** the process takes months or years.
- **People may save** extra income, particularly if they're worried or see it as a one-off.
- **Imports:** in an open economy like the UK, much spending goes abroad.
- **Crowding out:** if the government borrows, higher interest rates could cut private spending.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how an increase in government spending could lead to a larger increase in national income. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** The multiplier is the process by which an injection leads to a larger final change in national income. Its size is 1 ÷ (1 − MPC).

**Application:** If the government spends £1bn on new roads and the MPC is 0.6, the multiplier is 2.5.

**Analysis:** Construction workers and firms get the £1bn as income, and they spend 60% of it. That spending is income for other people, who in turn spend 60% of what they get, and so on. Each round adds to national income, with leakages making each one smaller, so the total rise is about £2.5bn.
:::

Why it scores: it calculates the multiplier, and explains the rounds of spending behind the number.
