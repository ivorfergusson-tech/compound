## Competition as a process

The models in this section describe equilibrium, but real markets keep moving. Firms are always trying to gain an edge, and rivals are always responding.

Competition brings benefits over two time spans.

**Short-run benefits:**

- Lower prices, as firms try to win customers.
- More choice and better service.
- Pressure on firms to cut waste.

**Long-run benefits:**

- New and better products.
- Lower costs from new methods.
- Resources moving to firms that use them best, so that the economy grows.

## Not just on price

Firms don't only compete by cutting prices. Competition also leads them to:

- **Improve products:** better features, design and reliability.
- **Reduce costs:** this lets them cut prices or earn more.
- **Improve the quality of service:** faster delivery, easier returns, better support.

These can matter more to consumers than a small price cut, and they're harder to copy.

## Creative destruction

You met [[creative destruction]] in 4.1.4. It's key to how competition works in a market economy.

The process runs like this:

1. A firm with monopoly power makes large profits.
2. Those profits give outsiders a strong incentive to get into the market.
3. Barriers stop them entering in the usual way, so they innovate to overcome the barriers, with a new product or a new way of doing things.
4. The new firm takes the market from the old one, and may then gain monopoly power of its own.
5. The process starts again.

So monopoly profit can be temporary, even with high barriers. A firm protected by its patents and scale can still be overtaken by a new technology that makes those advantages worthless.

## What this means for policy

This view changes how we judge monopoly.

A static view says a monopolist is inefficient because it charges more and makes less. A dynamic view adds that today's monopoly profit may be the reward for past innovation, and that the threat of creative destruction keeps a monopolist on its toes.

There are limits to this:

- Some barriers are hard to overcome, and the process can take a long time.
- A big firm may buy up small rivals before they become a threat.
- Workers and regions linked to old industries can lose out heavily.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how creative destruction can reduce monopoly power over time. (4 marks)

:::callout exam
**Knowledge:** Creative destruction is the process by which new technology and new firms replace existing ones.

**Application:** Photography firms that dominated the market for film saw much of that market disappear after digital cameras and smartphones arrived.

**Analysis:** A monopolist's large profits give outsiders a reason to find a way into the market. If barriers stop direct entry, they can innovate around them with a new product. Consumers switch, so demand for the monopolist's product falls and becomes more elastic, which cuts its power over price. So monopoly power can be temporary, even where barriers look high.
:::

Why it scores: it explains the incentive, the route round the barriers and the effect on the firm's power.
