## How Paper 3 works

It lasts 2 hours and has 80 marks, drawn from **all** of the course, both micro and macro.

- **Section A:** 30 multiple choice questions, worth 30 marks.
- **Section B:** an investigation, worth 50 marks. You get a set of data and extracts about a real issue, and questions that have been worth **10, 15 and 25** marks.

A sensible split is **about 40 minutes** on Section A, and **about 80 minutes** on Section B, as Section B needs reading time. At a mark every 90 seconds, Section A would take 45 minutes, so a little time saved there helps.

## Multiple choice tactics

- **Cover the options.** Work out the answer yourself first, then look for it. This stops a tempting wrong answer from misleading you.
- **Sketch it.** A rough diagram in the margin settles many questions on shifts in curves.
- **Read the small words.** Look out for **not**, **most likely** and **least**.
- **Rule out.** Cross off what's clearly wrong, then choose between what's left.
- **Check the working.** On calculations, wrong answers are often what you'd get from a common slip, such as using the wrong base.
- **Don't get stuck.** If a question takes more than two minutes, mark it, move on and come back.
- **Answer every question.** Never leave a blank, as a guess has a chance and a blank has none.

## The investigation

You're often asked to think like an economist advising a government or a firm. Spend real time reading first.

**Read with a purpose.** Read the questions first, so you know what you're looking for. Then go through the data, and mark key figures and any trends.

**Use the data carefully.** Good answers notice things:

- Trends, and when they change.
- Whether figures are real or nominal.
- Whether they're totals or per person.
- What's missing, or how old the data is.
- Whether two things that move together really cause each other.

**The 10-mark question** usually asks you to draw out what the data shows. Quote figures, and make a clear point about each one. Don't just copy numbers out.

**The 15-mark question** usually asks for economic analysis of a part of the issue. Use the chains from the long answer guide.

**The 25-mark question** usually asks you to make a recommendation or a judgement. Use the plan from the long answer guide, and bring in **both** the data and your wider knowledge. Micro and macro ideas can both be useful here, so show the links, such as how a tax on one market affects jobs and growth.
