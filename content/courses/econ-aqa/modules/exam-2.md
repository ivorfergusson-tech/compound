:::callout idea
Short data questions are the cheapest marks on the paper: be exact, use the data, and don't write more than the marks are worth.
:::

## Choosing your context

You choose one of two contexts. Don't pick by the topic alone. Look at the last, 25-mark question first, because that's over half of the section's marks. Pick the context where you could write the best answer to that.

## The 2-mark question

This is usually a calculation or reading from the data.

- **Show your working,** in case you make a slip.
- **Include the unit:** %, £ or £bn. A correct number with no unit can cost you a mark.
- **Check what's asked:** percentage change, or percentage point change? From which year to which?

**Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100**

## The 4-mark question

This usually asks you to explain a term or to explain something using the data. Aim for three things:

1. **Define** the key term precisely.
2. **Use the data:** quote a figure or a fact from the extract.
3. **Explain** what it shows, in a short chain.

Don't evaluate here, as there are no marks for it. Stop when you're done.

:::callout exam
**Sample question (ours, not AQA's):** Explain, using the data, what is meant by a concentration ratio.

**Answer:** A concentration ratio is the combined market share of the largest firms in a market. The data shows that the three largest firms have 62% of sales, so the 3-firm ratio is 62%. This suggests that a few firms dominate, which points to an oligopoly.
:::

## The 9-mark question

This usually asks you to explain something with the help of a diagram. There are three levels, with marks of **1 to 3, 4 to 6 and 7 to 9**. Top answers are well organised, with accurate knowledge, relevant application and clear chains of reasoning.

**Draw the diagram first.**

- Label both axes and every curve.
- Show the old and new positions, with arrows for the shift.
- Mark the new price and quantity.

**Then use it in your writing.** Name the curves and points as you go. A diagram that's never mentioned in your answer isn't doing its job.

A good plan for the writing:

1. Define the key term.
2. Say what changes, and why, using the extract.
3. Walk through the diagram: which curve shifts, and what happens to price and quantity.
4. Finish the chain: what this means for firms, consumers or the economy.

You need analysis here, and not evaluation, unless the question asks for it. Thirteen minutes is enough for a diagram and about a side of writing.
